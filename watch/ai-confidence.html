Why does AI sound so confident when it is wrong?

Watch the 75-second film, then read how to check each claim against its source.

English film and captions. AI-generated narration. The museum, website and answer are fictional teaching examples, not a test of a named product.

Read the film transcript

You ask an AI a simple question. The answer comes back polished, friendly, and completely sure. Here’s a made-up example. Is the Willowmere Museum open on Mondays? “Yes! It’s open daily, nine to five. And entry is free on Mondays.”

Now let’s check each claim against the museum’s own website. Nine to five. That matches. Open daily? No. It’s closed on Mondays. Free on Mondays? The website doesn’t say that anywhere.

So why did it sound so sure? Behind the answer is a language model. It writes by predicting likely next words, from patterns in huge amounts of text. Phrases like “open daily” appear on countless opening-hours pages, so the words fit. The same process got the hours right, and the day wrong. Sounding right and being checked are different jobs. And the way models are often trained and tested can reward a confident guess over saying, “I’m not sure.”

Search and links can help, but they aren’t proof on their own. A cited page may not say what the answer claimed. So here’s the habit. Ask for the source. Open it. Find the sentence that actually supports the claim. Confidence is a tone of voice. Evidence is something you can check.
