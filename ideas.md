# Where AI belongs in a business

Three questions to ask before using AI in a business. Which decision will it improve, who will check the result, and what does each accepted result cost?

These are the principles I use to explain and design AI workflows. They are supported by the linked essays and tools, rather than claims of measured company results.

## Start with the decision.

Name the decision, the person making it and the evidence it needs before choosing a model.

### Why it matters

A model is a component. A useful product also needs a person, evidence, a next action and a way to judge whether the decision improved. Starting there makes the limits visible before an experiment grows.

- Name the decision

- Identify the evidence

- Choose the next action

- Then test the AI

- Name the person responsible for the next action.

- List the evidence and what would make it insufficient.

- Choose an acceptance rule before comparing tools.

## Where the AI must stop.

Make the point for human checking, override or sign-off part of the product.

### Why it matters

An answer and an action carry different consequences. Property identity, missing information, permissions and accountability need explicit checks. A person must be able to see the evidence and stop or override the proposed action.

- AI proposes

- Check source and limits

- Person accepts or overrides

- Authorized action

### Use it tomorrow

- Identify the error that could affect the decision.

- Put the human check before that consequence.

- Show source, date and uncertainty at the check.

- Specify what happens when evidence or approval is missing.

## Cost per accepted result.

Include retries, failures and review when counting the cost of a result that meets an explicit acceptance rule.

### Why it matters

A cheaper request may require more retries or review. Count the full workflow cost against distinct results that meet an explicit acceptance rule. The cost ledger records caller acceptance; it does not independently certify the output.

- All attempt costs

- Include tools and review

- Count accepted results

- Divide cost by results

### Use it tomorrow

- Define what counts as one distinct result.

- Write the acceptance rule and who applies it.

- Record failed attempts, retries, tool costs and review time.

- Report no accepted results as undefined cost per result, not zero.
