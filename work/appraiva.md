## How does an observation become useful research?

A reviewer needs to know which property an observation describes and what other information supports it. Linking imagery, records and search is one way to make that context available within a research workflow.

The next step depends on the evidence. An image and a property record may describe different moments. Connecting them is useful only when a person can understand the context and identify what still needs investigation.

## Which questions help decide what to investigate next?

For illustration, consider a fictional research record: an image appears to show wear on a roof, while the property record is incomplete. These suggested questions would help decide what to investigate next:

- Is it the correct property? Check that the image and supporting records refer to the same property before combining their information.

- How current is the evidence? Compare image-capture and record dates; distinguish a past observation from information about present condition.

- What is observed, and what remains unresolved? Separate what appears in the image from claims that still need records or professional verification.

- What information is needed next? Identify the missing fact and appropriate follow-up before turning research into a recommendation.
