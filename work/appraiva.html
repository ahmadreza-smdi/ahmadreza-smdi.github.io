Decision: connect an image to property research

An image can show the condition of a property. Turning that observation into useful investment research takes more than an AI model.

Ahmadreza Samadi is a Dubai-based technology entrepreneur, Co-Founder & Technical Lead of Appraiva, which builds AI real estate intelligence, and Co-Founder & CEO of Royal Abraj Group. He has held the Appraiva role since October 2023 and works remotely from Dubai for the US company in Cleveland, Ohio. It builds AI tools that help real estate investors and developers find distressed and off-market properties through imagery analysis and enriched property data.

For property research, a condition signal needs context: which property it describes, what other information is available, and how a person can find and use it. Those questions connect model output with product design and engineering.

Responsibilities: connect imagery, data and search

Ahmadreza Samadi’s responsibilities connect imagery analysis, property matching, data enrichment, search, AI and cloud architecture. The work spans four connected areas:

  • Property discovery: connect imagery analysis to the property it describes.
  • Data enrichment: bring condition signals together with other property information.
  • Search: make that information available within an investor's research workflow.
  • Product and infrastructure: connect the interface, data systems, AI workflows and cloud services that support those steps.

The architectural challenge is keeping those connections useful: an image observation needs a property identity, supporting context and a clear next step in the product.

What does property data enrichment add to an image?

Property data can provide context for an image: which property it describes, when the information was recorded and what still needs to be checked. Appraiva’s founder announcement is an existing public reference for the company.

How does an observation become useful research?

A reviewer needs to know which property an observation describes and what other information supports it. Linking imagery, records and search is one way to make that context available within a research workflow.

The next step depends on the evidence. An image and a property record may describe different moments. Connecting them is useful only when a person can understand the context and identify what still needs investigation.

Which questions help decide what to investigate next?

For illustration, consider a fictional research record: an image appears to show wear on a roof, while the property record is incomplete. These suggested questions would help decide what to investigate next:

  • Is it the correct property? Check that the image and supporting records refer to the same property before combining their information.
  • How current is the evidence? Compare image-capture and record dates; distinguish a past observation from information about present condition.
  • What is observed, and what remains unresolved? Separate what appears in the image from claims that still need records or professional verification.
  • What information is needed next? Identify the missing fact and appropriate follow-up before turning research into a recommendation.
