Decision: give each system a clear purpose

A customer enquiry is the beginning of a workflow. The business still needs to understand the request, follow up, manage the work and keep the customer informed.

Ahmadreza Samadi is a Dubai-based technology entrepreneur, Co-Founder & Technical Lead of Appraiva, which builds AI real estate intelligence, and Co-Founder & CEO of Royal Abraj Group. I am Co-Founder & CEO of Royal Abraj Group in Dubai. The group covers property brokerage and documents-clearing services; I lead its digital strategy and technology.

In a general business workflow, each system needs a clear purpose. A website can explain a service and capture an enquiry. A CRM can support sales follow-up. Customer and operational tools can support case information, documents, responsibilities and delivery status.

These boundaries matter because a sales record and an active customer case answer different questions. Someone following up on an enquiry needs the next sales action. Someone delivering a service needs the current work, the responsible person and the information required to proceed.

The following system examples explain a general approach to business workflows.

Illustration: separate sales follow-up from service delivery

For illustration, system responsibilities can be separated as follows:

  • Website: explain the service and capture the enquiry.
  • CRM: organize lead information and the next sales follow-up.
  • Customer portal: support document handling and visibility into customer work.
  • Internal tools: connect case information, delivery status and team responsibilities.

A lead’s next sales action and a case’s next delivery action need to remain clear as work passes between people and systems.
