How does your phone turn text in a photo into copyable words?

A photo is a grid of pixels. Your phone can turn some of those pixels into selectable words using optical character recognition, usually called OCR. Modern systems often use machine learning to do that reading.

By Ahmadreza Samadi · 10 October 2026

The photo does not start as editable text

A camera records light and colour, not the letters a word processor stores. OCR is the bridge: optical character recognition converts text visible in an image into recognised characters.

Source: Google ML Kit: Text recognition v2

First, locate the text

The system identifies likely text regions and their positions. A recogniser can organise the result into blocks, lines, words and individual symbols. The boxes in the carousel illustrate this step; they are not a recording of one phone’s internal process.

Source: Google ML Kit: Text recognition v2

Then, recognise the patterns

Modern text recognisers use trained models to recognise character and word patterns in images. The result is text data, plus information such as where it appeared. Different tools can use different processing steps, languages and models. This simple sequence explains the idea, not one universal algorithm.

Source: Google ML Kit: Text recognition v2 · Google ML Kit: Recognize text in images on Android

Now you can select, copy or translate

A phone interface can place selection handles over recognised text and let you copy it into another app. Apple’s Live Text is one example. Translation, a website link or another action can happen after recognition; those are additional features, not the same task as reading the letters.

Source: Apple: Interact with text in a photo or video on iPhone

A clean photo gives the model a better chance

Blur, tiny letters, reflections and awkward angles can make recognition harder. Use enough light, keep the text in focus and photograph it clearly. Supported scripts and languages vary by tool, so not every recogniser reads every language.

Source: Google ML Kit: Text recognition v2 · Google ML Kit: Recognize text in images on Android

Copied does not mean correct

OCR can confuse similar characters or miss a small detail. Check names, dates, totals and reference numbers against the original before using them. Text recognition does not establish whether the document’s claim is true. Processing also varies by app: some recognition runs on the device, while other services can send an image to a server. Check the app’s privacy information before scanning sensitive material.
