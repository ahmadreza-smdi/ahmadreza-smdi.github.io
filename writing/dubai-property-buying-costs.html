Dubai property buying costs, item by item

By Ahmadreza Samadi · 20 October 2026

For a completed Dubai residential purchase, budget the price, the buyer-paid share of registration and service charges, any financing costs, agreed professional fees and property-specific settlement amounts. The headline registration percentage is not the whole cash requirement. Use the dated official fee references below, then reconcile each component with the actual agreement and written quote for your transaction.

Key points

  • Separate the total charge from the part allocated to the buyer.
  • Mortgage registration uses mortgage value, not automatically the purchase price.
  • Avoid stacking service-route fees or adding VAT to every component indiscriminately.

Which costs belong in the initial purchase budget?

Start with a component list rather than a single “all-in” percentage. For each item, record the amount or formula, the basis it applies to, who pays, when it is due and where the evidence comes from. Keep the property price separate from transaction costs. A mortgage advance is a funding source, not a discount on the underlying price or fee.

This guide concerns an ordinary completed residential purchase. Off-plan registration, commercial property, company or portfolio arrangements, seller financing and unusual ownership structures may use different processes. The word “full” means a complete budget for the identified transaction, not a universal schedule that covers every purchase. Use the appropriate official service and professional advice when the scope changes.

The Dubai Land Department sale-registration page lists separate seller and buyer shares of two percent each. It also lists document or map charges and service-partner fees. Confirm how the agreement allocates costs; do not assume that a buyer automatically pays every listed seller component. In the purchase-cost calculator, the payer share is an explicit scenario input rather than a hidden assumption.

Keep quoted estimates separate from paid amounts. Save the date and scope of each quote, and update the budget when the transaction instructions change. A document issued for a different unit, price, financing route or purchaser may not establish the charge for this transaction. The correct source and applicable basis matter as much as the numerical rate.

What do the official fee pages actually say?

The fee table below records official references opened on 20 October 2026. The DLD pages show a site-wide update of 14 October 2026, which is not a separate publication or effective date for every fee. Treat these as dated observations of the published service information. Confirm the applicable current charges with the actual registration route before committing funds.

The sale service differentiates service-partner charges around a price of AED 500,000. Its document and map entries are not a reason to add every alternative item to every apartment purchase. Ask which certificate or map is required and whether the quote already includes it. Preserve the route and item description so another person can reconcile the amount later.

For mortgage registration, the DLD service lists 0.25% of the mortgage value and additional document and partner charges, with different route descriptions. Keep that mortgage basis separate from the sale price. Also check whether a combined transaction quote already includes a registration or service component; adding the same charge again under a second label overstates the budget.

The Federal Tax Authority distinguishes residential and commercial supplies and describes different VAT treatment. Do not add a blanket five percent to the property price or assume that the property’s treatment determines every professional service. Where an official service page says a partner fee is plus VAT, retain that distinction and confirm the actual invoice treatment. This article is not an individual tax determination.

Which amounts must come from transaction-specific quotes?

Professional and financing costs require their own evidence. Record broker commission from the applicable written agreement, rather than treating a common market convention as a government tariff. For lender arrangement, valuation, insurance or settlement charges, use the lender’s documents for the specific product and borrower. This article does not quote a standard rate for those items or assess lending eligibility.

The DLD sale process identifies a developer no-objection e-certificate among the required documents for the relevant freehold areas. A requirement for a document does not establish a universal developer fee. Record any applicable amount from the actual issuing party’s quote, its payer allocation and whether it is already captured elsewhere in the settlement statement.

Property-specific amounts can include adjustments between the parties, existing obligations that must be cleared, deposits, immediate work and move-in or utility requirements. Not all are consumed transaction fees. A refundable deposit ties up cash but is different from a non-refundable professional charge. A prepaid amount may cover a future period rather than create an additional annual cost. Label each item accordingly.

Keep ongoing ownership costs visible but separate from completion costs. Building charges, maintenance, insurance and financing payments affect affordability after the transfer. Do not label a property inexpensive merely because the day-of-transfer cash is manageable. Equally, avoid counting an annual charge twice because part was prepaid or adjusted on completion. Reconcile the period covered by each payment.

How do you turn the list into a checkable cash requirement?

Create two totals: acquisition cost and cash required at completion. The first combines the price with applicable buyer-paid costs. The second also accounts for actual funding, deposits already paid and other settlement movements. State exactly what has been deducted. A reservation deposit is part of the payment path; subtracting it from the cash still due should not make it disappear from the total purchase cost.

Consider a fictional property priced at AED 1,000,000 with a mortgage value of AED 600,000. A buyer share of two percent of the price is AED 20,000; a buyer share of four percent would instead be AED 40,000 if the actual agreement allocated that total share to the buyer. The mortgage registration percentage component would be AED 1,500. These are arithmetic illustrations of different bases, not a transaction quote.

The example deliberately leaves partner, document, professional and property-specific amounts open. A completed budget needs the applicable evidence for those items. Entering zero because a quote is missing makes the final number look more complete than the evidence. Keep an explicit “quote needed” list outside the calculator and do not represent an illustrative subtotal as the cash required to complete.

Use the Dubai purchase-cost calculator to make the formulas and payer shares visible. Read its dated fee basis and exclusions before using the result. Then reconcile the printed inputs and output with the written transaction instructions. The tool performs the entered arithmetic; it does not verify the property, agreement, lender advance or applicability of a fee.

The practical output is a dated cost sheet with one source per material item, a list of unresolved quotes and a clear statement of who must confirm them. That sheet is stronger than an unqualified “price plus a percentage” rule. It helps the responsible people find missing evidence while there is still time to resolve it, without implying that an educational page has approved the transaction.
