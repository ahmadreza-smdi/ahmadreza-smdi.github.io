Why does AI sound so confident when it is wrong?

A polished answer can contain a correct fact, a mistake and an unsupported claim. Confidence in the wording does not tell you which is which.

By Ahmadreza Samadi · 5 October 2026

Updated 10 October 2026

At a glance

Polished wording does not prove that an AI answer is correct. Ask for a source for the specific claim, open it, and find the sentence that actually supports it. Check who published the page and whether it is current. A related link is not enough, and one answer can mix a correct fact, a mistake and an unsupported claim.

Watch the original explainer

75-second animated film in English with subtitles and AI-generated narration. The museum, website, chat and answer are fictional. Word suggestions illustrate the idea, not real model probabilities.

One answer, three different claims

In the film, an assistant says the invented Willowmere Museum is open daily, nine to five, with free entry on Mondays. Its invented website lists nine-to-five hours from Tuesday to Sunday, says Monday is closed, and makes no claim about free Monday entry.

The hours match. The day is wrong. The free-entry claim is unsupported. This is a constructed teaching example, not a test of a named AI product.

Plausible words are not the same as checked facts

A language model learns patterns in text and predicts likely next pieces of language. That process can generate accurate or inaccurate statements. NIST calls confidently presented false content confabulation. A fluent sentence is not evidence that its facts were checked. NIST profile.

Why a confident guess can be rewarded

Kalai and colleagues argue that common training and evaluation methods can reward guessing instead of acknowledging uncertainty. This helps explain some confident errors; it is not a claim that every model has the same training or behavior. Research paper.

Does the cited page support the actual claim?

A study of four generative search engines in 2023 found unsupported statements and citations that did not support their attached claims. Search and links can help, but you still need to inspect what the cited page actually says. Citation study.

How can you check a specific claim in an AI answer?

  1. Ask for the source. Request a page that supports the specific claim.
  2. Open it. Check who published it and whether it is current.
  3. Find the supporting sentence. A related page is not enough; it must support the actual claim.

Confidence is a tone of voice. Evidence is something you can check.
