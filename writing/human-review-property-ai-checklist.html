Where the person signs off: property AI review

By Ahmadreza Samadi · 20 October 2026

Put human sign-off at the point where an AI output would change a consequential property decision, not merely at the end of a document. Give the reviewer the underlying evidence, authority to stop or correct the action, and a place to record unresolved questions. The checklist below is a proposed workflow design, not a claim of company deployment or a professional certification.

Key points

  • Name the action and the person accountable for allowing it.
  • A readable source must support the specific claim about the specific property.
  • An unresolved item can block an action even when every form field is filled.

What exactly is the person being asked to approve?

“Review this report” is too vague to define a useful control. Write the action that approval would permit: include a property in an internal shortlist, send a statement to a client, change a record, or proceed to a separate professional process. Those actions have different consequences. A person checking wording should not be treated as having approved the valuation, legal status and financial assumptions embedded in the same paragraph.

Name the decision owner, the reviewer and the person who can execute the action. In a small team those may be the same person, but the responsibilities still need to be clear. If the reviewer cannot access the source or stop the action, adding a checkbox does not supply meaningful oversight. Make “return for more evidence” an ordinary outcome rather than a failure that people are pressured to avoid.

The NIST AI Risk Management Framework provides voluntary risk-management guidance across the AI lifecycle. It is a useful foundation for considering responsibilities, context and risk, not an approval badge for this checklist. The steps here translate that general concern into an editorial proposal for a property workflow. They do not establish compliance with a particular law, professional standard or lender policy.

Does the evidence support this property and this claim?

Start with identity. Match the building, unit or parcel identifier in the source to the property in the proposed decision. Similar names and matching unit numbers are not enough when the buildings differ. Keep the original identifier, the source’s wording and any documented crosswalk visible. If a relationship is inferred rather than established, record it as an open question.

Then separate observation, publication and access dates. A newly published page may repeat an old observation. A recent download does not make a floor-plan measurement current. Record the date relevant to the claim and explain any gap. Where the date is missing, leave it unknown. A reviewer should be able to see which facts were actually observed and which are simply being repeated.

Check the units and definitions before comparing figures. An internal area, a gross area and an area including a balcony describe different boundaries. Similarly, an asking rent is different from an achieved rent, and a market index is different from a valuation of this property. The source must support the precise sentence that will be used, not merely discuss the same topic.

NIST’s Generative AI Profile discusses risks including confidently generated false material and over-reliance. Its suggested actions include checking sources and citations. A source-looking link in an AI answer should therefore be treated as a lead to inspect. Opening a page confirms that it exists; it does not by itself confirm that the page establishes the claim.

Can the reviewer correct, reject or pause the output?

A useful interface gives the person more than an approve button. Show the claim and its evidence together, allow the wording to be corrected, and retain a clear way to pause the next action. If the system will send something externally, require the reviewer to see the final version and intended destination. Approval of a draft should not silently apply to a later changed draft.

Microsoft Research’s human–AI interaction guidelines address how systems should behave in ordinary use, when wrong and over time. That is design guidance for the interaction, not evidence that a particular property product is safe. For this proposed workflow, a practical application is to make the system’s limits and the person’s corrective options visible where the decision occurs.

Consider a fictional assistant that describes Unit 12 B as having two bedrooms. Its linked record is for a different building, also containing a Unit 12 B. The reviewer should be able to hold the statement and request the correct identifier rather than edit the number by guesswork. The existence of a complete note does not resolve the building mismatch, and a fluent explanation cannot supply the missing evidence.

Do not hide an exception by silently replacing the AI answer with a human guess. Record what changed, why it changed and which source supports the replacement. Where the evidence remains unresolved, change the action or leave the claim out. A record of a limitation is more useful than an apparently clean result that obscures the problem.

What should the sign-off record retain?

Keep a short record tied to the exact output version: the proposed action, property reference, material claims, supporting sources and dates, unresolved issues, reviewer decision and permitted next step. Record whether the outcome was approval, a narrower approval, return for evidence or rejection. This is a proposed record structure, not a requirement to publish private transaction information.

For each unresolved item, name an owner and a specific next check. “Verify later” is not actionable. “Compare the unit identifier with the current authoritative property record before sending the brief” identifies both the missing evidence and the action that must wait. A deadline can organise work, but it should not automatically convert an unanswered question into an approved fact.

Use the decision-first guide to define what should be built and where human judgment belongs. The Evidence Note tool can record a claim, source relationship, limitation and next check. Its recording checks do not verify truth, source identity or readiness to act. Keep that distinction visible when sharing or exporting a completed note.

Finally, test the handoff with a known exception before depending on it. Use invented records that contain an identifier mismatch, an old observation or incompatible area definitions. Check whether the reviewer can find the problem and stop the relevant action. Record what actually happened without claiming a performance rate from a few examples. The aim is an inspectable decision process, with a real person able to take responsibility.
