How does property data enrichment connect an image to the right property?

A property image becomes more useful when a reviewer can connect it to the correct property, inspect supporting records and see what remains uncertain. Property data enrichment brings those pieces together for further research.

By Ahmadreza Samadi · 10 October 2026

At a glance

Property data enrichment connects an observation with supporting property information. Check that the sources describe the same property, keep the dates and references attached, and make unresolved claims visible. Combining records helps organize a review; it does not turn an uncertain match or an unverified statement into a confirmed fact.

What information are we connecting?

An image observation answers a limited question: what appears in that image. A property record may add an identifier, a recorded characteristic or a dated note. The connection is useful when a person can follow each claim back to its source.

I would keep three things together: the property identity, the source of the information and the time it describes. That gives the reviewer a way to distinguish an observation from a recorded statement and decide what needs checking next.

Imagine a researcher receives Image A and Record B. Every identifier, date and observation in the table is invented for this teaching example. These are not customer records or an Appraiva result.

The first task is to establish whether P-17 and P-17B describe the same property. Similar labels alone do not establish that relationship in this example. Until supporting information resolves it, I would keep the records separate and mark the proposed match as unresolved.

How should the dates change the interpretation?

Suppose a reviewer later confirms that both records refer to the same property. The image still predates the work described in the note. An observation from March 2024 and a statement about April 2025 can describe different moments; they need not be competing accounts of the same condition.

The September 2026 update date does not tell us that the roof was inspected in September. It tells us when this fictional record was updated. I would keep that date separate from the date of the event the note describes.

The dark area is an image observation, not a diagnosis of damage. The roof-work statement is something the record says, not independent confirmation that the work happened. Neither source in this example establishes the roof’s present condition.

What would a useful combined research note say?

While the identity question remains open, a short research note could preserve the useful information without hiding the gap:

Proposed match: unresolved. Image A is labelled P-17 and was captured on 5 March 2024; a dark area appears on the roof. Record B is labelled P-17B and contains a statement about roof work on 7 April 2025. Its update date is 12 September 2026. Confirm the relationship between the identifiers and inspect the evidence behind the work statement before combining these into one property history.

This is a suggested note format, not a tested matching method. If the relationship is confirmed later, retain the source references and all relevant dates. If it cannot be confirmed, keep that limit visible rather than filling it with a plausible story.

The same principle can guide how results are presented in search: show the source and relevant date beside a claim, with unresolved information visible to the reviewer. This is a design suggestion, not a description of an existing product interface.

What would I check before using enriched information?

  1. Resolve the identity. Find supporting information that establishes which property each source describes. Hold an uncertain match for review.
  2. Read the dates with the claims. Separate image capture, the event described and the record’s update. Ask what information is needed to understand the property now.
  3. Keep the evidence and the next question. Attach the source reference, state what it supports and record what is still unverified. Choose the next check from that gap.

The useful output is a record someone can inspect and question. Adding more fields alone does not resolve a missing identity link or establish a statement’s accuracy.
