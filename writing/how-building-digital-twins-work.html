How can a building have a live digital copy?

An office can have a second version on a screen. The useful part is the connection: data from the physical building can update the digital model, helping people understand what is happening and where to look.

By Ahmadreza Samadi · 11 October 2026

Measure

Sensors collect readings from the building.

Connect

Each reading belongs to a room or piece of equipment.

Use

People inspect the updated model and decide what to check.

Give the real building a digital identity

The model represents spaces and equipment, including how they connect. A sensor can belong to Room 3, which belongs to Floor 2. These relationships make a reading meaningful: it is not just a temperature, but a temperature in a particular place.

Source: Microsoft Learn: Connect an end-to-end Azure Digital Twins solution

Sensors provide the changing part

Devices can measure temperature, energy use or other conditions. Software links incoming readings to the matching assets. A visual model can put that information beside the rooms and systems it describes. The readings and buildings in this carousel are fictional teaching examples.

Source: Autodesk Tandem: Features · Microsoft Learn: Connect an end-to-end Azure Digital Twins solution

Connected does not always mean instant

As new data arrives, the model can update. How often that happens depends on the sensors, connections and software. The illustrated change from 24°C at 09:00 to 28°C at 09:05 is not a real measurement or a promised update speed.

Source: Autodesk Tandem: Features · Microsoft Learn: Connect an end-to-end Azure Digital Twins solution

A clue can guide a maintenance check

If one room becomes unusually warm, the model helps a manager locate it and examine the cooling equipment serving it. The reading does not prove which part has failed. The benefit is context for the person making the decision.

Source: Autodesk Tandem: Features

A 3D tour and a connected twin do different jobs

A standalone static 3D model shows shape and layout. A building digital twin connects a representation to operational data. Some implementations also support simulations: for example, estimating what a different cooling schedule might do before changing the physical building. An estimate depends on the model and its assumptions.

Source: IBM: What is a digital twin?

Check whether the picture is still current

If a sensor stops sending data, its last value may remain on screen. An old number can look precise without describing the room now. Check the update time and data quality. The twin is a useful connected view, not an exact copy that knows everything or guarantees savings.

Source: Autodesk Tandem: Features · Microsoft Learn: Connect an end-to-end Azure Digital Twins solution
