How cold outdoor air can help warm a home

A fictional home and an illustrative energy balance explain how a heat pump moves heat.

Ahmadreza Samadi · 2026-10-20

English film with synthetic narration and English captions. Fictional caretaker and home; the heat courier illustrates energy transfer. Original animation, not product-test footage.

A fictional home, a real mechanism

Omar, the home and the heat courier are original illustrations. The courier represents energy transfer, not a literal object. This is an evergreen building-technology lesson, not a new product announcement.

Follow the sealed loop

A heat pump uses electricity to transfer heat from a source to a warmer space. Refrigerant absorbs heat outside. The compressor adds work, raising pressure and temperature; an indoor exchanger releases heat. Pressure then falls so the cycle can repeat. Refrigerant travels in the loop, not outdoor air. Three plus one is not free energy

Our made-up example collects three units of outdoor heat and uses one unit of electrical input to deliver four units of heat indoors. The four units have two sources. This is an illustrative energy balance, not a measurement or guaranteed coefficient of performance. IEA: How a heat pump works (2022 report; opened 28 October 2026)

Our building-fit questions

Where is the heat source? How large a temperature lift is required? Is the system sized and installed for this building? Performance depends on conditions, design and installation. Many systems can reverse to cool, but capabilities differ. The film promises no bill saving or payback and gives no installation instructions.
