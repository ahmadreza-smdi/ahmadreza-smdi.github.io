Why your phone puts you across the street

A fictional pickup explains reflected satellite signals, an uncertain map dot and a practical way to agree where to meet.

Ahmadreza Samadi · 2026-10-18

English film with synthetic narration and English captions. Fictional characters and map.

At a glance

A precise-looking dot can still be a location estimate. When it disagrees with your surroundings, compare a visible landmark and agree on a safe pickup point. Do not cross a road just to follow the dot.

Mina has not moved. Why has the dot?

In our fictional film, Mina waits between tall buildings. The location shown on her phone shifts across the street. The street, character and map interface are original illustrations, not a recording of a real journey or app.

A longer signal path

Google’s December 2020 engineering explanation describes buildings blocking direct satellite paths while reflected signals take a longer route. An uncorrected measurement can then place a pedestrian on the wrong side of a street. This is mechanism background, not a new announcement or a claim that every phone makes the same error. [Android]

The dot has other clues

A phone’s location is not necessarily GPS alone. Apple describes a service combining satellite, Wi-Fi, cellular and Bluetooth information with permission. Obstacles can restrict the satellite view. Its Maps circle indicates precision; it is not a promise about a particular journey. [Apple]

A pickup check you can use

Our proposed three-step check is: look, name, agree. Look at the actual pavement, entrances and signs. Name a visible landmark. Agree on the pickup spot in the app or with the driver through its normal channel. A clearer sky view may help, but do not enter traffic or a restricted area to obtain it.

What this does not prove

The story does not diagnose any particular phone. Hardware, signal conditions and software corrections vary. Google’s historical examples of mapping corrections are not universal performance claims for phones today. If an app displays uncertainty, treat it as information for a decision rather than proof of your exact position. [Android]

Start with the decision

The useful question is where you and the driver can safely recognize the same place. A clearly named meeting point can be more useful than arguing over which side of a street a dot appears on.
