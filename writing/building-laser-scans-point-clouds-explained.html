How does a laser scan become a building record?

R-CON’s organizer lists 12–15 October for a reality-capture conference. This is a timely reason to explain building laser scans, without claiming conference results.

By Ahmadreza Samadi · 14 October 2026

A laser helps measure distance.

A time-of-flight lidar measures the delay between an emitted pulse and its reflection. Many measurements can locate surfaces in three dimensions.

Check the record before the decision.

A laser helps measure distance.

A time-of-flight lidar measures the delay between an emitted pulse and its reflection. Many measurements can locate surfaces in three dimensions.

Source: USGS: Lidar glossary · Leica: Cyclone REGISTER

Many points form a point cloud.

Each point records a position. Together, points describe sampled surfaces. The dots are not automatically named walls, rooms or equipment.

Source: USGS: Lidar glossary · Leica: Cyclone REGISTER

One view leaves hidden surfaces.

An object can block the scanner’s view. More scan positions can add coverage. A realistic-looking view can still contain gaps.

Source: USGS: Lidar glossary · Leica: Cyclone REGISTER

Align the scans. Then check them.

Registration brings separate scans into a shared frame. Software can use targets or common geometry. An attractive alignment still needs quality checks.

Source: USGS: Lidar glossary · Leica: Cyclone REGISTER

Points are not a finished BIM.

Building Information Modelling adds interpreted objects and information. Turning points into that model requires choices and review. A scan alone is not a live digital twin.
